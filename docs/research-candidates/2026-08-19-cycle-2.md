# Stage 9A candidate selection — cycle 2

Status: pre-result selection record. No comparative candidate backtest was run or
inspected for this decision. Scores allocate research effort; they do not estimate
alpha or authorise a strategy. Cycle 1's closed `not_evaluable` consolidation result
was not relaxed, revisited, or used to select any candidate below.

Every candidate was independently operationalised, scored on the [model acceptance
standard](../model-acceptance-standard.md) scorecard, and then adversarially
verified against the actual codebase — not the docs — for data-feasibility
overclaims, latent implementation risk, and undisclosed overlap with its own batch
siblings. The verification pass changed the reading of three of five raw scores
below; those changes are recorded as findings, not silent corrections, consistent
with this project's erratum convention.

## Decision

Prioritise **Candidate A — SMA Cross v1, exposure-reduction estimand** for a
bounded, no-trade, per-asset first experiment, jointly designed against
**Candidate B — volatility-managed exposure**'s trailing-volatility-scaling rule as
its own required falsifier-beating control, not as a separately prioritised
candidate. Verification found that Candidate A's own preregistered falsifier
already requires beating Candidate B's mechanism; scoring and prioritising the two
independently would double-count one shared research question — does exposure-
gating on trend-state reduce risk more than exposure-gating on volatility level.
Candidate A also cleared verification with no data-feasibility overclaim and no
confirmed implementation-drift risk, which none of the other four candidates did
cleanly.

Candidates C (CTA v2) and D (cross-sectional rotation) remain eligible but each
requires infrastructure this codebase does not have today — a multi-instrument
pooled-portfolio weighting engine for C, panel/permutation statistical tooling for
D — before any protocol can lock, and both overlap materially with Candidate C's
own trend-family mechanism (see Cross-candidate findings). Neither is disqualified;
both are parked pending that engineering, not pending data. Candidate E (TA
Breakout v1) is not prioritised: the lowest raw score of the five, an explicit zero
on diversification, and the record's own admission that its distinguishing
mechanic degenerates to a CTA v1 retest once stripped out.

## Candidate A — SMA Cross v1, exposure-reduction estimand

| Field | Pre-result definition |
|---|---|
| Claim | Restricting a single long-only asset's exposure to sessions where the trailing 20-session SMA is above the trailing 50-session SMA produces materially lower realized annualized volatility and maximum drawdown than continuous buy-and-hold of the same asset, without requiring the rule to also raise compound return. |
| Scope | Each of the 12 locked ETFs independently; adjusted daily OHLCV from each asset's clean-history start; signal at close `N`, fill at open `N+1`; no cross-sectional or a-priori regime conditioning. |
| Mechanism | Sustained downtrends exhibit volatility clustering and negative return autocorrelation (leverage effect, forced-selling/deleveraging feedback); a lagging trend-state filter spends, on average, less time in the higher-volatility segment of the return distribution than continuous exposure — a return-distribution-shape regularity, not an information-arbitrage claim. |
| Market-belief proxy | Continuous buy-and-hold exposure — the implicit assumption that near-term risk is not predictable from a trailing trend-state indicator. |
| Reality proxy | The binary state `1{SMA20 > SMA50}` on completed closes, partitioning the sample into two realized-volatility/drawdown regimes. |
| Information set | Trailing daily OHLCV through completed close `N` only; fill at open `N+1`. `auto_adjust=True` rebasing means a re-fetch will not bit-for-bit reproduce historical crossing dates — a fingerprinting item, not an assumption to wave away. |
| Estimand | Per-asset and pooled `Δσ = σ(r_rule) − σ(r_bh)` and `ΔMDD = MDD(r_rule) − MDD(r_bh)` versus continuous buy-and-hold. Secondary, unscored: `ΔCAGR` and information ratio. |
| Alternatives | (1) A pure trailing-volatility-scaling rule with no directional component could produce similar drawdown reduction — this is Candidate B's mechanism, required as the discriminating control, not merely an alternative to note. (2) Concentration in one or two crisis episodes rather than a persistent regularity. (3) The 20/50 pair as a favourable draw, requiring a small preregistered neighbourhood grid. (4) Calendar coincidence. |
| Falsifier | `Δσ`/`ΔMDD` not distinguishable from zero under preregistered block-bootstrap inference; or the reduction is driven by 1–2 assets or one episode with no cross-sectional support; or Candidate B's vol-scaling control achieves equal or greater reduction, showing trend-state adds nothing beyond generic vol-timing. |
| Data feasibility | Fully available: Yahoo adjusted daily OHLCV for the 12-ETF universe, no fundamentals/macro/futures-roll required. Verified against `backend/app/fetch.py`: no overclaim. |
| Expression candidates | (a) No-trade / observation-only characterization of forward volatility and drawdown conditional on trend state — the required first bounded experiment. (b) Per-asset binary long/flat overlay, matching the existing unvalidated `SmaCross` prototype's mechanics. (c) Portfolio-level exposure scaled by the fraction of the 12 assets currently in the up-state. (d) Candidate B's trailing-volatility-scaled overlay, run as the control, not a separate candidate. |
| Path and risk | Whipsaw in choppy regimes raises turnover/cost drag — the primary channel by which naive MA-timing claims evaporate net of costs. Cash earns zero yield while flat per the benchmark convention, understating real T-bill carry. Maximum loss is bounded by the falsifier: worst case, the rule is strictly worse than buy-and-hold once whipsaw costs are included. |

**Verification finding.** Data-feasibility and every ADR citation verified
byte-for-byte against `backend/app/fetch.py` and the ADRs; no overclaim. Of the
three live implementations of SMA-cross logic (`strategies.py::SmaCross`,
`rules.py::build_rules`, `signals.py::compute_signal`), all three agree exactly on
window, shift, and crossing semantics — unlike CTA Trend and Donchian, whose
`rules.py` (level exit) and `signals.py` (crossing exit) implementations are
independently confirmed to disagree today. This candidate does not inherit that
drift risk. The one real gap: its own falsifier requires Candidate B's mechanism as
a control, which the raw score did not credit against Candidate B specifically
(only against CTA v1) — corrected by running both as one joint protocol rather than
two independently prioritised candidates.

## Candidate B — volatility-managed exposure overlay

| Field | Pre-result definition |
|---|---|
| Claim | Scaling next-day exposure to an ETF inversely to its trailing realized volatility, capped at 1x with no leverage, produces higher risk-adjusted outcomes (Sharpe, Sortino, Calmar) and smaller maximum drawdown than static buy-and-hold, net of costs — a risk-adjusted claim, not a total-return claim. |
| Scope | The 12-ETF universe, individually and as the equal-weight book, adjusted daily OHLCV from 2006; weight recomputed at each close, executed at next open. |
| Mechanism | Realized volatility is autocorrelated (clusters) and empirically elevated on the largest-drawdown days; cutting exposure through high-volatility stretches can smooth the equity path without any directional view — though it may be inseparable from mechanical vol-drag or disguised crash timing (see Alternatives). |
| Market-belief proxy | The implicit constant risk-per-dollar of a static, fully-invested position, which does not condition on volatility being autocorrelated. |
| Reality proxy | Trailing realized volatility from a preregistered fixed lookback/estimator, standing in for near-term risk being partly forecastable. |
| Information set | Adjusted daily OHLCV through close `N` only; fill at open `N+1`. No options-implied volatility exists in this codebase, so the market-belief framing is necessarily price-based, not a genuine forward-looking market expectation. |
| Estimand | Paired difference (scaled overlay minus static buy-and-hold, net of costs) in Sharpe, Sortino, max drawdown, and Calmar, per instrument and for the ETF-12 book, over the full sample and the 2008/2020/2022 windows. |
| Alternatives | Mechanical vol-drag (any vol-lowering rule raises Sharpe with zero genuine skill — requires a shuffled-timing placebo); disguised crash timing (vol and forward return are negatively correlated, so this may just be market timing); zero cash yield mechanically penalises every under-invested session; lookback/estimator/rebalance choice is itself an unlocked parameter family. |
| Falsifier | No material Sharpe/Sortino/Calmar/drawdown improvement once the shuffled-timing placebo achieves a similar improvement; or the benefit is confined to one crisis window; or the risk-adjusted gain requires a return shortfall beyond a preregistered tolerance. |
| Data feasibility | Trailing realized volatility and next-open execution are fully computable from existing adjusted OHLCV; correctly scopes a leveraged fixed-vol-target variant as out of scope under the no-leverage constraint rather than assuming it available. |
| Expression candidates | No-trade signal-quality study of whether trailing vol forecasts next-period vol/drawdown incidence. Per-instrument capped sleeve. Book-level application inside the ETF-12 basket. A fixed-vol-target levered variant is explicitly parked. |
| Path and risk | Turnover/cost drag from continuous weight recomputation; whole-share rounding creates tracking error against the intended continuous weight; zero cash yield biases the return-difference guardrail negative independent of the risk-adjusted claim; execution lag can leave the position stale exactly when volatility is jumping fastest. |

**Verification finding.** The raw-data claim is genuine — no futures, fundamentals,
options, or FRED data is smuggled in. But "implementable within existing simulator
mechanics" is false: `execution.py`/`rules.py` are discrete entry/exit state
machines with no continuous target-weight concept; `portfolio.py` sizing is
stop-distance risk-budgeted with no weight-target mapping; `portfolio_benchmark.py`
rebalances only annually and only ever buys on that branch. A weekly,
continuously-varying, per-instrument vol-scaled weight requires a new engine, not a
reuse of any of the three. The record also never pins a numeric `target_vol`, and
2008 — one of its own three headline evaluation windows — falls inside the
undefined volatility-lookback warm-up period with no stated policy. Its book-level
expression is deferred until that engine exists (see Cross-candidate findings);
its single-asset control role is retained inside Candidate A's protocol, where no
new engine is needed.

## Candidate C — CTA v2 (cross-asset volatility-scaled trend overlay)

| Field | Pre-result definition |
|---|---|
| Claim | A continuous volatility-normalized trend-strength signal — `(close − 252-day SMA) ÷ 20-day ATR` — combined long-only across all 12 ETFs into one diversified, vol-scaled portfolio has positive mean net-of-benchmark excess return, estimated as a single pooled portfolio-level statistic across all instruments and all walk-forward folds, not as 54 separate per-symbol candidates. |
| Scope | The 12-ETF universe, same 756/252/252-bar, 14-fold walk-forward structure as CTA v1; the estimand is computed on the pooled portfolio return series, not per-symbol. |
| Mechanism | (1) Gradual underreaction produces true own-asset trend continuation too small to detect from any single instrument in one year — pooling 12 instruments and 14 years raises the effective sample the test runs against; (2) continuously-sized, vol-scaled positions across asset classes capture a portfolio-level convexity effect from de-risking correlated losers — a property of the pooled portfolio, invisible in a single-instrument test. |
| Market-belief proxy | The vol-adjusted trend-strength score itself — the same information any trend-following participant already sees. |
| Reality proxy | Realized next-period vol-scaled excess return, aggregated across all 12 instruments and folds. |
| Information set | Adjusted daily OHLCV through close `N` for all 12 ETFs; fill at open `N+1`; no futures/fundamentals/FRED. |
| Estimand | Pooled mean daily net-of-ETF-12-benchmark excess return of one preregistered vol-scaled specification, tested against a one-sided circular block-bootstrap null with a small (2–3 variant) preregistered lookback family under Holm correction — replacing CTA v1's 54-candidate design, whose per-cell sample size could not detect a realistic IR≈1.0 effect. |
| Alternatives | Leveraged/tilted exposure to the benchmark's own drift, not timing (controlled via net-of-benchmark testing); volatility scaling alone mechanically raising Sharpe regardless of direction (controlled via a direction-blind placebo); the pooled result being driven by one or two crisis episodes; residual search risk from even a small lookback family. |
| Falsifier | The pooled bootstrap test fails to show material net-of-benchmark excess return; or the effect is attributable to the benchmark-drift or direction-blind placebos; or it is not stable outside the largest crisis episodes. |
| Data feasibility | Identical adjusted daily OHLCV and 12-instrument universe CTA v1 already used; no new data source. |
| Expression candidates | No-trade predictive-regression test of the pooled trend score. If cleared: a long-only, unlevered, continuously vol-scaled multi-asset overlay, weights summing ≤1. A binary-sleeve fallback if continuous weights prove unstable. |
| Path and risk | Continuous sizing raises turnover/cost drag beyond CTA v1's binary breakout; vol-scaling can overweight structurally low-volatility instruments (IEF, TLT), turning the overlay into an implicit duration bet; cross-asset correlations that normally diversify can collapse exactly when the overlay is most sized into losers. |

**Verification finding.** Data feasibility holds — identical to CTA v1's already-
used universe and source, no new data required. But the estimand's central
computational object — a single pooled, simultaneously cross-instrument-weighted
portfolio return series — has no existing implementation anywhere in this
codebase: `engine.py`/`strategies.py` run one symbol per `backtesting.py` backtest;
`execution.simulate()` replays one symbol; `research.py`'s
`CandidateWindowEvaluation` (CTA v1's own aggregation object) takes the *median* of
independently-run per-symbol backtests, never a concurrently-weighted portfolio.
This is a genuine build gap, not a parameter change. Separately, `rules.py` and
`signals.py` are confirmed to already disagree on CTA Trend's own exit condition
(level vs. crossing) — a live precedent for exactly the drift risk a new continuous
signal would be exposed to if hand-duplicated across both paths. This candidate
overlaps materially with Candidate B's variance-timing placebo and with
Candidate D's trend/momentum family (see Cross-candidate findings).

## Candidate D — ETF-12 cross-sectional relative-strength rotation

| Field | Pre-result definition |
|---|---|
| Claim | Among the 12 ETF-12 instruments, an asset's cross-sectional rank of trailing K-period return predicts its subsequent relative-return rank: top-third-ranked assets outperform bottom-third assets on a risk-adjusted basis, net of each asset's own absolute trend and asset-class-cluster membership. |
| Scope | The 12 ETF-12 names only, adjusted daily OHLCV from 2006; K and the holding horizon `K'` drawn from a small preregistered grid; long-only, no leverage. Does not extend to the ~500-symbol equity list, which remains unusable for any historical cross-sectional test. |
| Mechanism | Capital reallocation across asset classes may lag the macro-regime shift that justifies it — large pools rebalance slowly, mandates are sticky, flows chase realized performance with a delay — producing multi-month relative-return persistence across asset classes. Distinct in form from CTA's time-series signal, but draws on the same underlying regime persistence a time-series filter also detects; not assumed orthogonal to it. |
| Market-belief proxy | Trailing K-period cumulative total return per instrument, expressed as a cross-sectional rank/z-score. |
| Reality proxy | Each instrument's realized relative total return over the subsequent `K'` periods. |
| Information set | Adjusted daily OHLCV through completed close `N` for all 12 instruments simultaneously; `auto_adjust=True` rebasing means a rank computed today is not guaranteed bit-identical to what would have been computed live — must be captured by a data fingerprint. |
| Estimand | Primary: the population cross-sectional relationship (Spearman rank correlation or panel-regression coefficient) between trailing and subsequent rank, net of own-trend and cluster membership. Secondary, contingent: net active return/risk of a top-`M` sleeve versus Passive ETF-12 v1. |
| Alternatives | Generic absolute trend (top-ranked names are very often also in an absolute uptrend, highly correlated by construction with only 12 names); asset-class-cluster beta rotation (6 of 12 names are US-equity-linked, so "top-ranked" can trivially mean whichever regime — risk-on equity, or flight-to-quality duration/gold — currently dominates); rebalancing-flow/calendar effects. |
| Falsifier | The rank-continuation coefficient is not material once controlling for own-trend and cluster beta, or a cluster-shuffled permutation null shows the effect is indistinguishable from a null built on the same cluster structure. Also falsified as `not_evaluable` if a prospective check shows too few effectively independent rebalance-period observations. |
| Data feasibility | Adjusted daily OHLCV for all 12 names already exists with no survivorship/PIT gap; asset-class-cluster labels also already exist as data. Effective cross-sectional breadth (roughly 4 clusters inside 12 names) is unverified as sufficient for a cluster-controlled estimate and must be assessed prospectively. |
| Expression candidates | No-trade detector/feasibility study with a prospective power/breadth assessment. If it survives: a long-only top-`M` overweight sleeve, equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly or quarterly, versus Passive ETF-12 v1. |
| Path and risk | Rebalance turnover cost from names oscillating across the top-`M` boundary; concentration risk if the sleeve collapses onto the 6-name US-equity cluster; regime-reversal/whipsaw risk immediately after rebalance. |

**Verification finding.** The raw-data claim (12 clean ETFs, no survivorship gap,
cluster labels already present in `portfolio_universe.py`) is genuine. But the
estimand's required *methodology* — a cluster-controlled panel regression plus a
cluster-shuffle permutation null — has no implementation anywhere in this
codebase: `research.py` contains only a single-series circular block bootstrap;
`backend/requirements.txt` carries neither `scipy` nor `statsmodels`. This is an
overclaim by omission in the "data feasibility" field, which covers required
transformations, not only raw data existence. Separately unaddressed: with only 12
names split 4/4/4 at `M=3–4`, a tie in trailing return (plausible for TLT vs. IEF,
or GLD vs. DBC) will be resolved by whatever default tie-break the rank
implementation uses, and can swap a quarter of the sleeve's composition. Overlaps
materially with Candidate C (see Cross-candidate findings).

## Candidate E — TA Breakout v1

| Field | Pre-result definition |
|---|---|
| Claim | When price forms a resistance zone from at least two confirmed local pivot highs that has rejected upward penetration at least twice, a completed daily close beyond the zone's upper boundary, entered at next available open, is followed by higher forward N-day cost-adjusted returns than a regime/volatility-matched control. The multiple-rejection/pivot-confirmation mechanic is the load-bearing, falsifiable difference from a generic new-high breakout; stripped out, the claim degenerates into retesting CTA v1. |
| Scope | The locked 12-ETF universe only for any backtest; the ~500-symbol list may support forward-live detection on currently-listed names only, never a historical backtest, for the same survivorship reason that parked Cycle 1's cross-sectional-momentum candidate. |
| Mechanism | A multiply-tested, multiply-rejected level is one where standing supply has repeatedly absorbed demand; a confirmed close through it is evidence that supply has been consumed. Persistence could come from resting stop-buy orders and breakout-following capital clustering above a well-known level — a microstructure/attention story that must be discriminated from plain trend continuation (CTA v1's already-rejected mechanism) and volatility-compression release. |
| Market-belief proxy | The zone's upper boundary, established from at least two confirmed prior pivot highs, representing what the market currently treats as a defended ceiling. |
| Reality proxy | The qualifying breakout itself — a completed close beyond the zone by a locked buffer, sustained through a time-acceptance filter. |
| Information set | Only OHLCV through the completed close of bar `N`; pivots require an explicit `K`-bar confirmation lag so no future bar leaks into zone construction; fill at open `N+1`. |
| Estimand | Stage A/B: mean difference in forward N-session return, maximum adverse excursion, and maximum favourable excursion between qualified events and a matched control. Contingent strategy-level estimand: net-of-cost excess CAGR/max drawdown/Calmar versus Passive ETF-12 v1. |
| Alternatives | Generic trend continuation (already DonchianTrend/CTA v1); volatility compression/expansion; broad regime drift, especially on correlated members (SPY/QQQ/XLK); detector/parameter-search selection across the pivot/tolerance/lag/buffer/horizon grid. |
| Falsifier | Breakout-event outcomes are statistically indistinguishable from a matched control, and separately indistinguishable from an otherwise-identical test using the existing `DonchianTrend` prototype's raw rolling-high definition in place of the multi-touch zone — isolating the multi-touch mechanism specifically even if generic breakout effects exist elsewhere. |
| Data feasibility | Feasible for the 12-ETF universe only; not feasible as a historical backtest on the ~500-symbol list. Only total daily session volume exists — no order-flow/volume-at-price data — so any volume filter is a coarse approximation and must be labelled as such. |
| Expression candidates | No-trade Stage A/B detector and event study, mirroring the Cycle 1 structure. Long-only entry with one of four locked stop families. A tactical overlay partially reallocating benchmark exposure toward currently-breaking-out assets. Explicitly out of scope: any historical scan over the ~500-symbol list. |
| Path and risk | Gap risk dominates given next-open fill after a volatility-compressing zone; failed-breakout whipsaw is a defining risk of this specific mechanism; correlated simultaneous breakouts across overlapping members (SPY/QQQ/XLK) create concentration the equal-weight benchmark does not anticipate. |

**Verification finding.** Data feasibility is the most carefully-hedged section of
the batch and survives scrutiny with no overclaim. But adding this mechanism would
require the same three-way duplication pattern (`strategies.py` /
`rules.py` / `signals.py`) already confirmed to have caused a real, live
divergence for CTA Trend's exit condition — and this candidate's mechanics (zones,
confirmation lag, tolerance, four stop families) are more complex than any
currently-implemented strategy, multiplying that surface. The record's own text
concedes that stripping its distinguishing mechanic degenerates into a CTA v1
retest, which the score's `distinct_information` reflects but which was not
checked against Candidate C specifically.

## Cross-candidate findings

**Infrastructure gap.** Three of five candidates — C fully, D fully, B's book-level
expression — require a genuinely new pooled or panel computational engine that
exists nowhere in this codebase today:

- A simultaneous, cross-instrument weighted-portfolio return series (needed by C,
  and by B's book-level expression). Every existing backtest path
  (`strategies.py` via `backtesting.py`, `execution.simulate()`,
  `research.py`'s `CandidateWindowEvaluation`) is single-instrument or a median
  across independently-run single-instrument backtests, never a concurrently
  weighted portfolio.
- Panel/cluster-controlled regression and a permutation/cluster-shuffle null
  (needed by D). `research.py` has only a single-series circular block bootstrap;
  neither `scipy` nor `statsmodels` is a project dependency.

Neither gap disqualifies its candidate; both are engineering prerequisites that
must be scoped and either built or explicitly deferred before any protocol locks —
discovering this now, before preregistration, is the intended value of checking
data-feasibility claims against code rather than narrative.

**Overlap and double-counting risk.** Not yet reflected in any individual score
above:

- A and B share one research question from two conditioning angles (trend-state
  vs. volatility-level); resolved by running them as one joint protocol.
- C's own alternatives section names B's variance-timing mechanism as a rival
  explanation for its own result; if both proceed independently, this placebo must
  be shared, not separately re-litigated.
- C and D are both trend/momentum-family signals on the same 12 names; their
  pooled portfolio returns would likely be highly correlated in any trending
  regime, so scoring both as fully independent evidence of "trend exists" would
  overstate the family's effective trial count.
- E's own record concedes that its distinguishing mechanic, once stripped, is a
  CTA v1 retest — the same family C also sits in.

Any future attempts-ledger entry for C, D, or E must record these as a dependent
trial group, not independent draws, consistent with the exploration protocol's
dependence-group requirement.

## Benchmark suitability

Candidate A's primary comparator is buy-and-hold of the same instrument, not
Passive ETF-12 v1 — it is a single-asset risk-shape claim, not a portfolio-vs-
passive claim. If Candidate A's no-trade first experiment clears its gate and an
executable expression is proposed, Passive ETF-12 v1 becomes the relevant
comparator for any book-level or portfolio expression, subject to the ADR 0005
suitability audit as with every other candidate. Candidates C and D are direct
Passive ETF-12 v1 comparisons by construction and inherit its suitability audit
without further justification at this stage.

## Update (2026-08-20): Candidate C (CTA v2) picked up directly

Picked up from this record without minting a new cycle, the same precedent Cycle
5 set for Day-of-Week. Two corrections to how this record should be read, both
recorded here rather than silently applied:

1. **Engineering cost, corrected.** `portfolio_execution.py` and `portfolio.py`
   (committed 2026-08-18, the day before this record's original Cross-candidate
   findings section was written) already implement a real shared-cash,
   multi-symbol, sector/cluster-capped daily replay engine. It was built for the
   live "Today" view (discrete whole-share sizing, stop-distance risk) rather
   than Candidate C's continuous vol-scaled target-weight estimand, and is not
   wired into `research.py`'s bootstrap pipeline — so the "no simultaneous
   cross-instrument weighting exists anywhere" framing above is stale. What is
   actually required is a weight-vector construction and portfolio-return
   aggregator, not a from-scratch engine; the significance test itself needs no
   new bootstrap machinery at all — see point 2.
2. **Channel 1's rationale, re-read against the 2026-08-19 audit
   ([docs/audits/2026-08-19-methodology-and-implementation-audit.md](../audits/2026-08-19-methodology-and-implementation-audit.md)).**
   That audit's finding was not "trend following doesn't work" — it was that
   CTA v1's 54-candidate-per-fold design was underpowered (minimum detectable
   effect ≈ IR 4.1; power ≈ 2.5% at a realistic IR 1.0), so its null is
   `insufficient evidence`, not `evidence of absence`, per
   [model-acceptance-standard.md:78](../model-acceptance-standard.md). Candidate
   C's own estimand — one pooled series across all 12 instruments and 14 years,
   tested once, instead of 54 independent per-fold candidates — is the direct
   methodological remedy to exactly that power problem, a fact this record
   already stated before the audit existed ("replacing CTA v1's 54-candidate
   design, whose per-cell sample size could not detect a realistic IR≈1.0
   effect"). Channel 1 is therefore reinforced, not undermined, by the audit.
   Channel 2 (vol-scaled de-risking as an *independent* source of value) is
   genuinely weakened by SMA Cross v1's result: a pure volatility-only placebo
   with zero trend information matched or beat the SMA-state rule's variance
   reduction on 12/12 assets. Channel 2 is retained in the protocol below only
   as the required shared placebo control this record already named as
